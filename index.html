Format guide

Shooting in Dubai: know before you go

A few rules catch first-time filmmakers out. Check current details with the Dubai Film and TV Commission at filmdubai.gov.ae before you shoot, because requirements and fees change.

Public places need a permit

Shooting on streets, beaches, parks and government-managed sites generally needs a permit from the Dubai Film and TV Commission. Landmarks and some venues may need extra approval.

Drones are separate

Aerial filming needs its own aviation approval on top of any location permit. Never fly without it.

Private spaces need consent

Homes, studios and venues are simpler, but get the owner’s agreement in writing first.

Respect privacy

Don’t film identifiable people, especially women and families, without their clear consent. Privacy rules in the UAE are strict.

Student and personal films

Some personal or educational shoots may be treated differently, but confirm for your exact plan before relying on that.

Agree credits early

Write down who does what, who gets credit and who owns the finished film before the first shoot day.

Questions first-timers ask

Short answers to the things that stop people from starting.

I’ve never made a film. Can I join?

Yes. Mark yourself as “Just starting” on the People board. Many short films need a helper, a runner or an extra pair of eyes, and that is how most people learn.

Do I need my own equipment?

No. A modern phone is enough for a first short film. Add a cheap clip-on mic and good light and it will look much better.

What if nobody applies to my project?

Make the logline sharper, list fewer roles, and keep the film under five minutes. Small, clear projects attract people far more than huge ones.

Who owns the film?

Decide before you shoot. A one-page note that lists roles, credits and who can show the film is enough for a first project.

This is a starter prototype. Sign-in, a shared database, messaging and approved-access scripts are the next build step.
